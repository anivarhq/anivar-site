It watches. It listens.
It remembers.

A free, open-source alternative to Blue Iris. Anivar records your security cameras around the clock and understands what they see and hear — the faces you teach it, number plates, breaking glass — then answers your questions about it. It's an ordinary app for your computer, and all of it runs there.

What it does

Recording is the easy part.

Anivar also knows what it's looking at. Every model runs on your computer, and none is downloaded until you ask for it.

Every second, on your own disk.

Each camera records continuously to files on your computer, within a disk budget and retention you set. Scrub to any moment — events are pinned on the timeline.

  • USB webcams, RTSP, MJPEG, and ONVIF discovery
  • Sub-second live view for IP cameras over WebRTC
  • Export a clip, or an incident report with its video

Faces you teach it. No one else.

Enrol someone once and Anivar names them when they appear. A name needs several frames to agree before it's used, and people it doesn't know wait in Review for you.

  • Face recognition runs on your machine
  • Person re-identification links sightings across cameras
  • Remove a person and their biometrics are erased

Plates read. Cars remembered.

Number plates are read on-device and matched to the names you give them. A car that keeps coming back is flagged, so you can decide who it is.

  • Plate reading plus body colour
  • Known plates shown by name
  • Vehicles get their own feed in Review

It hears what the camera hears.

On cameras that carry sound, audio is classified against 521 everyday sounds — glass, alarms, barking, a raised voice. Most must be heard twice in a row before they alert, so one bang isn't an emergency.

  • YAMNet sound classification, on-device
  • Top three sounds and loudness saved with each event
  • Sounds get their own feed

Ask for what you remember.

Search by what was in the frame — “red shirt”, “person with a package” — or ask in plain words. The answer comes from your recordings first, with the clips attached. When there's nothing there, it says so instead of guessing.

  • Image search over every detected object
  • A language model that runs on your computer — or your own OpenAI, Anthropic, Gemini, Groq, xAI or LM Studio
  • Every answer says which hours it covers

Alerts only when it matters.

Draw zones and fence lines on each camera, then choose what earns an alert: entering a zone, loitering, climbing a fence, running, someone falling, a crowd. Alerts go to your own Telegram bot, with quiet hours and per-category mute.

  • Motion masks for trees, fans and screens
  • Browse people, vehicles and sounds from Telegram
  • Share a clip as a signed link that expires

How it works

Everything happens on this computer.

There is no Anivar server. The app on your desk is the whole system — recording, recognition, search and the assistant.

Your computer
  1. CamerasUSB, RTSP, MJPEG, ONVIF
  2. RecordEvery stream written to your disk
  3. DetectONNX Runtime on your GPU or CPU
  4. UnderstandFaces, plates, sounds, behaviour
  5. RememberA database and video files in your app folder
  6. AnswerSearch, and a language model on your machine

Only these ever leave the computer — and only if you switch them on:

TelegramoptionalAlerts and clips, sent to your own bot.
A cloud AIoptionalOnly if you add your own API key.
TailscaleoptionalViewing links over your own tailnet, with an expiry.

Private by construction

Your footage never needs to leave.

There's nothing to sign up for and no Anivar server to send anything to.

Models when you ask

Download

Get Anivar.

Free, open source, and yours to keep. Windows is where it's developed and tested day to day; macOS and Linux builds come from the same code.

Windows

Windows 10 or 11 · 64-bit

Download

Anivar-windows-x64-setup.exe

Installs for you only — no administrator prompt. Uses any DirectX 12 GPU, and NVIDIA cards through CUDA.

macOS

macOS 11 or later · Apple Silicon

Download

Anivar-macos-arm64.dmg

Intel Macs are not supported.

Linux

64-bit · Ubuntu 22.04 / Debian 12 or newer

Download AppImage

Anivar-linux-x86_64.AppImage

Other package formats

.debDebian · Ubuntu .rpmFedora · RHEL · openSUSE

Models run on the processor. The AppImage is portable — chmod +x and run.

Every file has a SHA-256 checksum published beside it — release notes and checksums. To update, open Settings → Check for Updates in the app.

These early builds aren't signed by a certificate authority, so Windows and macOS each ask once before opening them. What to click.
